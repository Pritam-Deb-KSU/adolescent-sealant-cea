## 1. Response to Reviewer (Major concern 4)

**Comment:** "Please include a probabilistic sensitivity analysis with cost-effectiveness acceptability curve especially for an implementation scenario."

**Response:** We thank the reviewer. We added a probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA; 10,000 iterations) varying all uncertain inputs jointly (Methods, Sensitivity Analysis), with cost-effectiveness acceptability curves for the three cases (new Figure 3c). The PSA supports the base-case results: the cost per averted decay was $37.60 for Case 1 (base case $37.46), and every case was cost-effective in more than 95% of iterations at a willingness to pay of $68 per averted decay, about half the cost of restoring one tooth. For the implementation scenario, Case 1 was modeled with an uncertain consent rate centered on the approximately 15% reported for 2M programs in our survey. Because consent scales sealant and averted filling costs proportionally, the implementation curve is identical to that of Case 1; consent changes the size of the benefit (75 averted 2M decay per 1,000 adolescents at a mean consent rate of 15%), not the cost-effectiveness.

## 2. Text to add to the manuscript

**Methods – add at the end of the "Sensitivity Analysis" paragraph (103 words)**

We also conducted a probabilistic sensitivity analysis (10,000 iterations): retention, attack rates, and sealant and filling costs were drawn from their Table 1 distributions, and dental visit probabilities were scaled by a triangular factor (0.8, 1.0, 1.2). Expected rather than randomly simulated transitions were used, so results reflect input uncertainty only. Cost-effectiveness acceptability curves give the proportion of iterations in which willingness to pay per averted decay, times averted decay, exceeded the net program cost (Fenwick et al., 2001). An implementation scenario delivered Case 1 with an uncertain consent rate, beta(2.25, 12.75), with mean 15%, the approximate 2M-program consent rate in our survey.

**Results – new paragraph after the Figure 3 paragraph (103 words)**

In the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, the cost per averted decay was $37.60 (95% uncertainty interval [UI] $12–$68) for Case 1, $39.47 ($16–$65) for Case 2, and $44.75 ($21–$71) for Case 3. Each case was cost-effective compared with no sealants in more than 95% of iterations at a willingness to pay of $63, $62, and $68 per averted decay, respectively (Figure 3c), about half the cost of restoring one tooth. As consent scales sealant and averted filling costs proportionally, the implementation scenario matched Case 1’s curve; at a mean consent rate of 15% it averted 75 2M decay (95% UI 12–187) per 1,000 adolescents.
